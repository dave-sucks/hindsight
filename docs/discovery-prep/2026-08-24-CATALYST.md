## 2. The play — calendar-first, Play D second

The review wrote "Play A (catalyst calendar)". Playbook Play A is *theme → people → names*, and
that is the wrong shape here: **for a dated-binary seat the candidate universe IS a calendar** and
is fully enumerable from primary sources. The 2026-08-09 session proved it — the wide calendar
sweep produced 6 usable names, the social orbit turn produced 0.

**Running order:**

1. **Perplexity / primary calendars lead** (P1–P3) — build the candidate list. Supplemental and
   label-expansion PDUFAs live in FDA dockets, sponsor 8-Ks and press releases, not on X.
2. **Grok second (Play D — reuse the bench)** (G1–G3) — the qualitative layer only: *is the date
   real, what is the residual risk, how crowded is it.* Do not ask Grok to discover the names.
3. **Trader convergence is a caution flag, not confirmation.** This seat's winners (XENE, ARQT,
   VRDN) were boring de-risked drifts; its losers were the crowded coin-flips. Use convergence to
   ask "why is this still cheap?", never to rank.

**Expected failure mode, called in advance:** supplementals are *less* covered on X than first
approvals — the excitement is on novel molecules. If G1–G3 come back thin, that is **not** a bad
window and **not** a broken prompt. It is the beat being quiet, which is exactly the boring
low-variance corner we are trying to buy. Log it in FUNNEL.md and proceed on the calendar names.

**Sourcing window.**
- **Primary — catalyst dated 2026-10-01 → 2026-12-31.** 60–90+ days out, per DAV-214. These
  become the entry queue for late Sept / Oct.
- **Secondary — catalyst dated 2026-08-31 → 2026-09-21, supplementals only.** These are the ones
  that can actually contest a slot with IONS/MIRM right now.

## 3. Perplexity Finance prompts — fire these FIRST

> Each is date-stamped. Perplexity needs it or it recycles prior-year catalogs.

### P1 — the supplemental / label-expansion calendar (the main event)

```
Today is August 24, 2026.

List US-listed companies with a DATED FDA decision in the window October 1, 2026 through
December 31, 2026 where the decision is a SUPPLEMENTAL action on an ALREADY-APPROVED
product, not a first approval of a new molecule. Include all of these action types:

- sNDA / sBLA (supplemental new drug or biologics application)
- label expansion or new indication for a marketed drug
- new patient population (pediatric, adolescent, expanded age or severity band)
- new formulation, new route of administration, or new dosing regimen
- PDUFA goal dates on products already generating revenue
- Advisory Committee meetings scheduled on a supplemental application
- CHMP opinions expected on an already-US-approved product

For each row give me: company, ticker, approximate market cap, the exact decision date,
the review class (standard 10-month vs priority 6-month), the acceptance/filing date,
the product and what is already approved about it, and the primary source for the date
(FDA docket, company 8-K, or press release with its date).

Label market cap and sponsor size, do not filter on them. Include every row you can date.
If a date is reported but you cannot confirm the primary source, keep the row and mark it
"unverified" rather than dropping it.
```

### P2 — the single-asset exclusion (this is the gap-risk filter)

```
Today is August 24, 2026.

For each of these companies: [PASTE TICKERS FROM P1]

Tell me, per company:
1. How many products it currently has FDA-approved and commercially marketed.
2. Trailing twelve-month product revenue, and what share of it comes from the single
   largest product.
3. Whether the upcoming FDA decision is on that same lead product or on a different one.
4. Whether the company is profitable, and its cash runway in quarters.

Then classify each as SINGLE-ASSET (one approved product, or one product driving >80% of
revenue, or clinical-stage with no approved product) versus DIVERSIFIED. I am deliberately
avoiding single-asset sponsors because a negative FDA outcome there gaps the stock 20%+
overnight rather than costing 5-8%.
```

### P3 — the variance profile (score what a CRL actually costs)

```
Today is August 24, 2026.

For each of these companies: [PASTE TICKERS FROM P1]

Give me the historical downside evidence for the specific event ahead:
1. What did this stock do on its own prior FDA decision days (last 3 years) - single-day
   percentage move, up and down, and whether each was a first approval or a supplemental.
2. Current options-implied move for the expiry covering the decision date, if available.
3. Analyst price targets: consensus, high, low, and how many of them already assume the
   supplemental approval.
4. Percentage of 52-week range the stock currently sits at, and distance from its 52-week high.

Then rank the names from lowest to highest expected downside on a negative decision.

Separately, for context: across biotech generally, what is the typical single-day drawdown
on a Complete Response Letter for a SUPPLEMENTAL application on a marketed drug, versus a
CRL on a first approval for a clinical-stage company? Cite examples from the last 3 years.
```

For each: company, ticker, event, exact date, the primary filing or announcement it comes from,
and current deal spread or expected move if applicable. Source from SEC EDGAR filings and index
methodology announcements, not from social commentary.
