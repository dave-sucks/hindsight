## How a look ends

Every look ends with one note, and the note carries your verdict: what the evidence since your last look did to the belief as written. A verdict is about the belief, not the stock, so news that backs a bearish belief makes it stronger. A move in the price alone leaves it unchanged, because the triggers and the floor already answer the price.

Two things on the stock are yours to keep current, and you change them in that same note.

Conviction is how confident you are in the belief. Move it when the evidence moved your verdict, and leave it when it didn't. A belief that broke usually leaves conviction low.

Outlook is what you expect of the stock from here: bullish, neutral or bearish. Look at it again whenever you re-read the case, or the price has moved a long way since it was set: after a report, on news, at a review, when a buy or a sale fires. A belief that broke is rarely still bullish, and on a stock we hold it usually means proposing the sale. Leave the outlook as it is when nothing has changed what you expect. A stock whose line says its outlook is not rated yet gets one from you at this look.

Give each change its reason in a sentence. The next look checks that reason against the day.
