A report. A trigger on this company's earnings report fired: the report is coming, or it just landed.

Before the report:
1. What is expected, and what has the stock done into it?
2. Which number, or which assumption, does the report have to clear? Your note sets that bar.

After the report:
1. What did they report against what was expected? Judge a surprise on an estimate under $0.05 a share in dollars.
2. What did management say about what comes next? Read the call before trusting a number.
3. How did the stock take it? The reaction is information.
4. Which assumption does it touch, and did it clear the bar?

A stock we hold. Before: size the position for a 10% move either way from the last close. Trim, hold through, or raise the floor. After: a broken assumption is a sale, so propose it. A beat with guidance raised: raise the floor, and add if the playbook allows one here. A beat the stock fell on: raise the floor.

A stock we watch. Before: the buy waits for the report. After: the case is stronger and the playbook's buy is true today: propose the buy. An assumption broke: stop watching, or send it to the Writer. Otherwise move the buy price to where the chart now puts it, or leave it.

Done: one note. Before, it names the bar. After, it gives the report against the bar, how the stock took it, and the verdict.
